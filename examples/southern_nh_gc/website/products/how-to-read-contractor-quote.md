# How to Read a Contractor Quote

**A homeowner's guide to deck, garage, and addition estimates**  
O'Sullivan Construction & Property Management · Southern New Hampshire

---

## What a real quote includes

A professional quote is more than a single number. At minimum you should see:

1. **Your name and project address**
2. **Scope of work** — what is included, item by item
3. **Exclusions** — what is *not* included (paint, interior finish, landscaping, etc.)
4. **Price** — total and/or line items
5. **Timeline** — start window and estimated duration
6. **Payment terms** — deposit %, draw schedule, final payment
7. **Validity date** — quotes expire (30–60 days is normal)
8. **License and insurance** — GC license number; proof of insurance on request

If any of these are missing, ask before you sign.

---

## Two pricing formats (both are valid)

### All-in per square foot (common for decks)

Example: *"192 sqft composite deck — $21,500 installed"*

**Good when:** The project is well-defined (size, height, railing type known).

**Check that scope lists:** footings, framing, decking, railing, stairs, permits, cleanup.

### Materials + labor (common for garages and additions)

Example:

| Line | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Materials package | $28,400 |
| Labor package | $31,200 |
| **Total** | **$59,600** |

**Good when:** The shell is complex and material prices move with the market.

**Check that materials list:** lumber, concrete, roofing, siding, hardware — not vague "materials."

## Line items to look for

### Decks

- Demo and disposal of old deck (if applicable)
- Footings below frost line
- Ledger and flashing at house
- Framing, decking, fascia
- Railing and stairs
- Permit fees and inspections
- Site protection and final cleanup

### Garages

- Excavation and base prep
- Slab or frost wall foundation
- Framing, roof, siding
- Door **opening** (door itself may be extra)
- Dried-in shell definition
- Permit and inspections

### Additions

- Foundation and structural tie-in
- Framing and roof integration
- Exterior envelope (siding, windows, roof)
- What interior finish is **excluded**

## Red flags in a quote

| Red flag | Why it matters |
|----------|----------------|
| No written scope | Disputes later — "I thought that was included" |
| Cash-only, no contract | No recourse if work stops |
| 50%+ deposit upfront | Standard is often 10–33% to start |
| No license number | Required for NH GC work over certain thresholds |
| Vague "we'll handle permits" with no fee line | Permit costs and responsibility should be clear |
| Price far below other bids | Missing scope, unlicensed subs, or change-order trap |
| No change-order process | Every extra becomes a fight |

## Comparing two quotes

Never compare on **total price alone**. Compare on **scope**.

1. Print both quotes side by side
2. List every task (footings, permits, railing, etc.)
3. Mark ✓ if included, ✗ if missing, ? if unclear
4. Ask each contractor to clarify ? items in writing
5. *Then* compare totals

A higher quote with complete scope is often cheaper than a low quote plus change orders.

## Deposits and payments — fair terms

- **Deposit:** Enough to lock the schedule and buy long-lead materials — not the whole job
- **Progress payments:** Tied to completed milestones (foundation done, dried in, etc.)
- **Final payment:** After walkthrough — hold enough to ensure punch-list gets done
- **Change orders:** Written and signed before extra work starts

---

## 10 questions before you sign

1. What exactly is excluded from this price?
2. Who pulls permits and schedules inspections?
3. What happens if materials cost more before we start?
4. How do change orders work?
5. What is the warranty on labor? On materials?
6. Are you licensed and insured in NH? (get license #)
7. Who is on site day to day — you or a lead carpenter?
8. What is the estimated start date and duration?
9. What do you need from me before start (access, utilities, decisions)?
10. Can I have a copy of the signed contract and scope?
